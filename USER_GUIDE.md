# Glance Platform

Glance puts independent, movable desktop tiles under one manager. Glance Platform 2.0 includes Finance, LLM Usage, Plex, Investments, Weather, Storage, System Resources, Cameras, SABnzbd, qBittorrent, Messages, Clock, Notes and the optional Liquid Icons tile, plus custom desktops, with shared options, styles and right-click menus.

## Start

Windows 10/11 with .NET Framework 4.8 is required. Download **Glance-Platform-2.0.0-Setup.exe** from the release page. If Glance is running, choose Quit Glance before running Setup. Setup installs for your Windows account and offers a desktop shortcut. Glance appears in Windows **Installed apps / Add or remove programs**. Uninstall removes program files and shortcuts while keeping saved tile data.

For a portable copy, download the Windows ZIP, extract the entire folder, and run Start Glance.cmd. Keep Glance.exe, Glance.TileSdk.dll and the other files together. The included CPU sensor component requires Windows administrator permission. Setup installs or updates it only when needed; if you decline that prompt, Glance still installs, and you can add CPU temperature later with Settings → Repair CPU temperature. Portable copies request permission on their first normal launch.

Open Glance from the Start menu or its desktop shortcut. If another app starts Glance inside its own private copy of AppData (some developer tools do), Glance says so and doesn't open, so it never works on a separate copy of your tiles.

Try Glance.cmd opens a separate sample workspace. Its Finance, Usage and Plex tiles use synthetic data. Your normal workspace is separate.

## Add and manage tiles

Browse Marketplace to install a bundled tile or import a .glancetile file. Enable installed tiles in My Tiles. Marketplace currently uses bundled packages and a local catalog; public hosting and automatic internet tile downloads are planned.

**Options** opens the tile's options window, with its own content controls followed by shared Style and Desktop tabs. The **···** menu has the same commands, in the same order, as the tile's right-click menu, followed by **Tile setup** (live or sample data mode where supported; changing it restarts the tile) and **Remove tile**.

Drag a tile to move it. Right-click for lock, pin, refresh and options. Finance supports multiple chart windows in one tile package. Usage and Plex have no host title bars.

In Platform's **Desktop** tab, **Lock All Tiles** keeps every installed tile in place, including all Finance charts. **Unlock All Tiles** lets you move them again. These choices are saved for tiles that are currently off too. Locking doesn't stop updates or change the layout.

Resize an unlocked tile, or a Finance chart, by dragging any side or corner to any size and shape, so tiles fit wherever you want them (on a custom desktop too); the side you drag snaps exactly to nearby tiles and screen edges. Each tile lays its content out to the size you give it: bars and text follow the width, and rows spread out on a taller tile (Weather shows fewer days on a shorter one, at least three; Investments and Plex show as many as fit). A tile can't be made smaller than what keeps its content whole. To make the text and pictures bigger or smaller instead, **hold Ctrl and drag a corner**: the tile zooms as a whole, from 75% to 200%, keeping its shape. Right-click → **Size to fit** gives a tile the size its content needs. Moving a tile snaps it to other tiles, the screen edges, the divide between monitors and the taskbar. Lock position also locks the size. You can also scale tiles in their options: Finance → Layout → Scale each tile (or right-click a chart → Tile actions → Tile scale); Usage → Display; Plex → Playback → On Your Tile, then Save changes. Clock, Notes, Investments, Weather and Storage have their Tile scale slider under Desktop. Each setting is saved independently.

Minimize or close the manager to leave tiles running beside the clock in the system tray. Double-click the tray icon to restore the manager. Choose Quit Glance from the tray to stop all tiles.

## Connect data

- Finance retains charts, portfolio records, local calendar events, saved setups and its original appearance controls.
- LLM Usage shows your Codex and Claude subscription limits. Connect only the providers you want in its Options (Accounts → Account setup). Codex and Claude are checked every minute by default (from 10 seconds to 15 minutes), and the tile respects the services' retry deadlines.
- Plex shows who is watching, with viewing history, CPU/GPU indicators and optional Tdarr pausing. Set up its Plex and Tdarr connections in its Options (right-click the tile → Options…). The Tdarr section shows CPU and GPU temperatures when available: GPU readings come from Windows, and CPU readings come from the included Glance CPU Sensor service on supported AMD Zen and Intel PCs. No separate monitoring app is required. If setup was cancelled, use Settings → Repair CPU temperature. Temperatures show Celsius to the left of Fahrenheit; unavailable readings stay hidden. Avoid giving two running Glance Plex instances control over the same Tdarr work.
- Investments shows your Webull balances, holdings and reported Open P/L. Connect Webull using your own approved US API access in its options.
- Storage shows each internal and USB drive with a usage bar and its free space. Bars turn amber at 80% used and red at 90%; a wide tile also shows the percent used. Its options can hide drives, leave out USB drives and change how often free space updates.
- System Resources shows CPU, memory, GPU, network and disk activity in one compact tile, with the same readings as Task Manager. Bars turn amber at 75% busy and red at 90%, and the top of the tile names the busiest resource. Each disk gets a short bar with its drive letter. Its options choose which rows to show and how often they update (every 2 seconds by default).
- Cameras shows live video from cameras on your home network. In its Options, add each camera with a name and its IP address (Tapo app: tap the camera → ⚙ → Device Info), or choose **Find cameras**, then enter the camera account (Tapo app: camera → ⚙ → Advanced Settings → Camera Account; not your TP-Link password). Tapo cameras need Third-Party Compatibility turned on (Me → Tapo Lab). Each camera gets its own tile: drag it anywhere, and drag a side or corner to give it any size and shape. Options → **Picture** chooses what a box of another shape shows: the **whole picture** with black bars (the default) or **fill the box** with the edges cut off; right-click → Tile actions → **Fit to the camera's picture** gives the tile the camera's shape again. The video is decoded on your graphics card when your PC can, which takes far less of the processor (Options → **Video decoding** can keep it on the processor; the tile's status in Glance says "graphics card" when it is used). To put them all in one tile, right-click → Tile actions → Put all cameras in one tile (or Options → Tiles). Double-click a camera's picture for full screen on its monitor; double-click again, or press Esc, to put it back where it was. The video goes straight from the cameras to your PC, and it pauses while the tile is hidden. Battery-powered cameras that only stream through their app can't be shown. To watch or save what a Tapo camera recorded on its SD card, enter your TP-Link account password (the Tapo app sign-in) in Options → TP-Link Account, then right-click a camera → Tile actions → Recordings…. Saved recordings go to Videos → Glance Cameras as .ts files (VLC and Windows Media Player play them).
- SABnzbd shows what SABnzbd is downloading and what finished or failed. With SABnzbd on this PC it needs no setup: it reads SABnzbd's address and API key from SABnzbd's own settings. Click **Downloading** or **Completed** to switch lists; double-click a finished download to open its destination folder. Right-click a download to pause it, change its priority or category, move it, rename it, save its NZB file, open its **Download options** (name, password, category, priority, unpacking, script) or delete it. Right-click a finished download to open it or its destination folder, see Windows' own file menu (More options from Windows…), **Force redownload**, save its NZB file, rename it, see its details, **Remove from SABnzbd (keep the files)**, **Delete files from this PC (keep in SABnzbd)…**, or **Delete from SABnzbd and this PC…** (its folder goes to the Recycle Bin). Right-click the title bar for the tile's menu, or a tab to pause or resume all downloads or open SABnzbd in your browser. Make the tile big (about 640 x 330 or more) for the **full layout**, like a download program: toolbar, filters by status and category on the left, a sortable table, the selected download's General, Files and Report tabs below, and a status bar. Double-click the tile → Options → **Look** for the layout (automatic, compact or full), text size, row spacing, the details line, progress bars and their color, tidier names and clock times. For SABnzbd on another computer, double-click the tile → Options, enter its address (for example 192.168.0.5:8080) and its API key (SABnzbd → Config → General → API Key), then **Test connection** and **Save changes**.
- qBittorrent shows qBittorrent itself in a tile: its own Web UI, live from the qBittorrent running on your PC, so the list, filters, details, right-click menus and adding torrents work exactly as in qBittorrent, which can stay minimized. On this PC it needs no setup. If the tile says it can't reach qBittorrent, turn on its Web UI: in qBittorrent, **Tools → Options → Web UI → Web User Interface (Remote control)**. Double-click a torrent to open its folder. The ⋯ button on the title bar has **Open download folder**, **Open qBittorrent in your browser** and, while qBittorrent is closed, **Start qBittorrent**. It looks like the SABnzbd tile and takes the tile's colors; for qBittorrent's own look, or its menu bar (File, Edit, View, Tools, Help), right-click the title bar → Options… → Look. The Status / Categories / Tags list on the left is turned on from qBittorrent's **View** menu. While the tile is hidden its page rests (it stops asking qBittorrent for news and uses less memory) and wakes as soon as the tile shows.
- Messages sends texts from your PC through your Android phone (Google Messages for web in a tile). The first time, click **Sign in** in the tile and sign in with the Google account your phone uses; your phone then asks you to confirm. Then pick a conversation, type, and press Enter. To send a picture, drag it from File Explorer onto the conversation, or press **Win + Shift + S**, select an area, click the message box and press **Ctrl + V**. Your phone needs Wi-Fi or mobile data; it doesn't need a cable. The ⋯ button on the title bar has Reload, Open in your browser, and Unlink your phone from this PC.
- Weather supports city or postal-code search worldwide, current conditions and a 10-day forecast (as many days as fit the tile, at least three). Choose the matching location and your preferred units in its options. It uses Open-Meteo without an API key.

Fresh installations have no accounts or personal data. Tile data is stored locally under %LOCALAPPDATA%\Glance\Platform\data. The sample workspace uses PlatformTryout. Removing a tile retains its private data for recovery.

## Custom desktops

A custom desktop gives one monitor its own arrangement of tiles and programs, swapped in with a keyboard shortcut. In the Glance window open **Desktop**, scroll to **Custom Desktops** and choose **New desktop**. Name it, pick the monitor, click the shortcut box and press the keys you want (for example Ctrl + Alt + 1), and choose a background. Use **Add tile** and **Add program** (programs come from the windows that are open; tick **Start this program when it isn't running** if you like), then **Show desktop**, move and resize everything where you want it, and click **Save arrangement**. From then on, press the shortcut to show the desktop and press it again to go back: every window returns to where it was. Right-clicking the background also offers to leave. Each monitor has its own desktops (or choose **All displays** for one desktop across every monitor); Windows' own Win + Ctrl + arrow desktops still work alongside.

Your normal desktop can be kept the same way: in the **Desktop** tab, **Save arrangement** remembers where every tile is, its size and zoom, which tiles are hidden, and where each camera window is; **Restore saved** puts all of that back. While a custom desktop shows, Save arrangement saves that desktop instead. **Bring tiles onscreen** brings back any tile that ended up off screen.

## Update and back up

Open **Settings → Platform updates → Check for updates**. If a newer stable release is available, choose **Install and restart**. Glance downloads from its public GitHub release, verifies SHA-256 checksums and program versions, waits for tiles to finish cleanup, and restarts with your original workspace. Installed copies use the installer; portable copies update in place with a backup for rollback. Existing Glance desktop shortcut icons refresh, and custom workspace arguments are preserved. Updates are started by you; Glance does not silently install a new Platform version.

If any tile cannot confirm cleanup, the update stops and its recovery files are kept. Update logs and portable program backups are under the workspace's updates folder. Update folders older than two weeks are removed when Glance starts. Quit any other workspace using the same program folder before updating. Back up %LOCALAPPDATA%\Glance\Platform before making major changes.

Use **My Tiles → Update all installed tiles** to update every installed tile with a newer bundled or local catalog package. Enabled tiles restart after cleanup; disabled tiles stay off. Settings, placement and private data are preserved. A failed tile update does not prevent other tiles from updating. Add a creator's newer .glancetile through **Marketplace → Add to catalog**, then run Update all. Online tile hosting remains future work. Platform upgrades apply newer bundled tiles on restart when each tile's automatic-update preference is enabled; Update all installed tiles also lets you apply them manually.

## Create tiles

To make a tile without writing code, use **Create Tiles → Create with AI**: describe what you want, choose **Copy AI prompt**, and paste it into any AI chat. When the AI replies, choose **Build from AI reply…** and paste the reply. Glance compiles it, adds it to your Marketplace, and lets you review what it can access before installing. If the build fails, copy the fix request back to the AI and try again. AI-written tiles run with your Windows account's access, like any tile.

Open Create Tiles in the manager, or extract creator-kit.zip. The kit contains the SDK, a starter project, Clock/Notes examples and the creator guide. Native tiles run with your Windows user's permissions; install packages from creators you trust.

## Known limits

Glance's installer and portable app are unsigned. Online marketplace hosting and publisher verification are still planned. Simulated tests cover Tdarr recovery; this release was not verified by controlling a live encoding queue or rebooting Windows.
CPU support installs as Glance CPU Sensor in Windows Installed apps, separately from the per-user Platform app. Its elevated service runs from Program Files and only shares CPU temperature with local readers. Uninstall Glance CPU Sensor there if no longer needed; its uninstaller preserves the shared PawnIO driver for other programs. Unsupported sensors remain unavailable; no Windows security setting needs to be disabled.


## A shared look for your tiles

Right-click a tile and choose **Options…**, then **Style**, or choose **Style…** directly from its menu. The **Layout** list offers ready-made looks: **Classic** (the rounded Glance presentation with roomier details, on black), **Compact Grey** (dense rows, a flat surface and small headings on the theme's grey), **Compact Black**, **Midnight**, **Charcoal**, **Outlined**, **Neon** and **Paper**. A look sets the layout, background, border and corners together. Change any of them and the list shows **Custom**. Every look keeps your information available.

Choose the background, corner shape, border style, colour and thickness. The preview changes first; **Apply to this tile** saves only this tile. To give every installed tile the same appearance, including tiles currently turned off, use the **Style** tab in the Glance window and **Apply to all tiles**. The Style tab says a style was applied only after Glance has saved it, and tells you if it couldn't. Applying a style leaves accounts, notes, locations and desktop positions unchanged. You can customize a tile independently afterward.

**Merge Snapped Tiles** is on by default for the bundled tiles. With snapping enabled, drag two tile edges together: the touching borders dissolve and their corners fill in over one second. Only the outside edges remain outlined, with matching curves around inward corners. Pull a tile away to restore its own frame. Tiles keep their individual colours, sizes and content; use **Apply to all tiles** for a uniform surface. Under **Merge Animation**, choose **Follow Windows**, **On · 1 Second**, or **Off**. On enables the effect only in Glance even when Windows animations are disabled. High contrast still skips the transition. Turn merging off in Style to retain separate frames.

Bundled tiles use Finance-style options with a branded header and horizontal tabs. Movable tiles have **Style** and **Desktop** tabs after their own content controls. Desktop controls (lock, always on top, snap, click-through, opacity) save immediately and change only that tile. Use the Glance window's Desktop tab to change every tile.

### Master settings for every tile

The Glance window's **Style** and **Desktop** tabs are the master settings. Choose a look or desktop behavior (lock, always on top, snap, click-through, opacity) and **Apply to all tiles**: every installed tile gets it, including disabled ones, and tiles you install later start with it. A tile's own **Style** and **Desktop** tabs change only that tile, so one tile can still look or behave differently; each has a button that opens the master tab. **Settings** holds the theme, Start Glance with Windows, the desktop shortcut, your workspace, CPU temperature repair and Platform updates. **About** shows the Platform version, every installed tile with its version, links to the release notes and this guide, and where Glance keeps its data.

### Liquid Icons

Install **Liquid Icons** from Marketplace and enable it in My Tiles. It is a separate optional tile that puts liquid backgrounds behind your existing desktop icons and labels: oil, water, glass and more. Nearby backgrounds flow together. Windows still handles opening, dragging, selection, menus and renaming.

Open Liquid Icons **Settings**. Everything is on one page with a live preview; nothing changes on the desktop until you choose **Save Changes**. A new install starts with the **Oil Puddle** look: black oil with a wavy edge and a few drops.

On the right, **Look** is a list of starting points. Choose one, then adjust anything; once a value differs from every look, the list says **Custom**.

- Oil and water, with a wavy edge and drops: **Oil Puddle**, **Water Spill**, **Black Drips** and **Clear Water**.
- Glass, with a smooth edge and a bright line on it: **Glass**, **Frosted** and **Smoked**. Glass doesn't read the wallpaper, so animated wallpapers keep playing underneath.
- Solid, smooth liquids: **Obsidian**, **Mercury**, **Pearl**, **Ocean**, and **Ink** (a plain black fill with no lighting).
- **Neon**: dark glass with a colored glow.

Under the list are the color and every value of the look:

- **Solid Body** runs from clear (0%) to solid (100%). **Highlights** are the bright reflections on the edge; on a pale liquid, where white can't show, they also shade the edge around them. **Reflections** lays soft patches across the middle: bright ones show on a dark liquid, darker ones on a pale liquid. **Bright Edge** is a thin bright line right on the outline, as on glass.
- **Edge Depth** makes the edge look rounder and deeper: it darkens the side away from the light and, on a dark liquid, lightens the side toward it. **Edge Thickness** is how wide the rounded edge is; the edge values show more on a thicker edge. **Shadow** falls away from the light. **Glow** surrounds the liquid in its own color (the small square picks it) and tints the bright edge.
- **Wavy Edge** gives a puddle outline, **Drops** scatters small drops beside the liquid, and **Drips** lets it run down. 0 turns each off.

Under the preview are the settings every look shares. **Light And Edge**: the light direction, opacity, edge softness (0 is a crisp outline, 16 a wide soft one) and join reach (how far apart icons still flow together; 0 keeps them apart). **Shape**: Rounded, Pill or Square, roundness, width and height. **Motion**: **Drop Jiggle** is how strongly a dropped icon snaps into the pool and jiggles: while you drag, its liquid lifts away from its neighbours, and when you drop it, it snaps in and wobbles (click the preview to replay it; 0 turns it off). **Settle** is how long the liquid takes to glide into place after icons move, from a quick snap (50) to a slow glide (800); changing it replays the drop in the preview. Unticking Animate Connections turns motion off.

Disable Liquid Icons or use **Hide All Tiles** to remove its backgrounds; the wallpaper and icon positions remain yours. The surface stays attached to the desktop, so it has no movable frame, pinning or global tile style controls, and Lock All Tiles does not lock your Windows icons.

Requires Windows 11 version 24H2 or newer (Settings → System → About shows your version). Windows 10 and earlier Windows 11 draw the wallpaper and the icons as one layer, with no room for backgrounds in between; there My Tiles shows "Needs Windows 11 Version 24H2 Or Newer". It reads icon rectangles only, keeps positions in memory, and does not read shortcut targets or file contents. If the desktop layout is unsupported, its status explains that and no backgrounds are drawn.

The right-click menu follows the same order across tiles. Finance's chart controls and Notes' text-editing commands are under **Tile actions**. Every menu in Glance and its tiles has the same look, with Windows 11's rounded corners.
